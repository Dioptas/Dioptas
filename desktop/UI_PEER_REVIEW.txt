## New findings

All of these come from reading code and static screenshots; I didn't reproduce them at runtime.

- **Q1. Legend text can be crossed by curves of the same color (minor; visualization and readability).**
  - **Seen in:** map-compact-large.png and batch-compact-large.png. Placement moved the legend to the bottom-right, so the primary peaks and the A cursor are now clear. But the green "CeO2_Pilatus1M" entry sits on the green overlay's narrow peaks, and the peaks visibly cut through the letters in batch-compact-large.
  - **Cause:** `.plot-legend` has no halo or backdrop (`plotLegend.css:1-16`). Placement samples only about 128 points per curve (`PatternView.tsx:419`), so narrow diffraction peaks can fall between samples.
  - **Fix that keeps your choices:** a 1-px dark text-shadow halo, and counting each sampled segment's maximum rather than isolated samples. The legend would stay compact, inside the plot and movable, with no header.
- **Q2. Placement doesn't update when the cursor or ROI moves (minor, possibly intentional).**
  - **Where:** the placement calculation's dependency list (`PatternView.tsx:471-489`) leaves out `linkedPosition` and `regions`, although both are used as obstacles (`:450-463`).
  - **Trigger:** move the A cursor or a map ROI into the legend's current corner. The legend isn't re-placed until the data or layout next changes.
  - If this is meant for stability, a comment would make that explicit.
- **Q3. Clicking the legend can pin it without telling you (minor).**
  - **Where:** any pointer move while the pointer is captured calls `move()`, which sets manual mode (`PlotLegend.tsx:67-70, 160-165`).
  - **Trigger:** click the legend to focus it so the long names wrap, with a small hand jitter. The legend is now pinned and stops adjusting automatically until Home or Escape, and nothing shows it's pinned.
  - **Fix:** require a few pixels of drag before switching to manual mode.
- **Q4. Native Batch window size isn't remembered (unresolved native evidence).** See `current-placement.log:1` above. I can't tell from the log whether this is a product defect or a test-readiness problem, so I haven't counted it as confirmed. I did let it affect `settings_files`.
- **Q5. Bound messages use an ASCII hyphen (trivial).** They read "≈ -6.283185 rad", while the rest of the UI uses a Unicode minus (`numericDraft.ts:15`).

## How I separate 9.0 from 9.5

- **9.0:** in the reviewed workflows the category's key expectations are met and I saw no material defect. At least one minor limitation is still visible, or the category's main behavior hasn't been exercised end to end.
- **9.5:** everything in 9.0, plus I couldn't find any limitation beyond optional enhancements, and executed behavior checks in both headless engines cover the category's main expectations.
- **10:** needs broad empirical validation, meaning native-platform runs and real users or assistive technology. No category qualifies.

**What would justify the next step:**
- **To 9.0** (the categories below 9):
  - Responsiveness: a completed native-GPU benchmark tied to a source commit.
  - Settings and files: a passing native Batch smoke, including window-size restore.
  - Maintainability: splitting `App.tsx`'s `run()`, keyboard and save-gating logic into owners like `useDraftReplacement`.
  - Accessibility: a VoiceOver or NVDA pass over the main workflows.
- **From 9.0 to 9.5:** fix that category's listed minor limitations (e.g. Q1–Q3 for visualization, or a visible next-undo label for reversibility) and add an executed check for it.
- **To 10:** native multi-platform qualification plus user or assistive-technology validation.

# Addendum to my 59fd3e38 review: legend refinements and new performance evidence

**Bottom line:** Q2, Q3 and Q5 are fixed. Q1 is only partly fixed: the halo works, but same-color curves still show between the letters, and in one standard-size screenshot the default legend sits over the main curve. I found no regressions. My scores are unchanged and the mean stays **9.02**.

As before, this was read-only. I didn't open `UI_DESIGN_REVIEW.json` or anyone else's scores, and I didn't execute anything.

## Q findings

| ID | Status | What I checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1** Legend text crossed by same-color curves | **Partly fixed** | **Fixed:** `plotLegend.css:11-15` adds a 1-px halo in the workspace color, with no panel or header. `legendSampleIndices` keeps each bucket's min and max (`legendPlacement.ts:58-80`), so a single-sample peak isn't skipped; `pattern_samples.test.mjs:101-108` tests this. **Still visible:** in map-compact.png, map-compact-large.png and batch-compact-large.png, the green "CeO2_Pilatus1M" entry still has green peak lines running between its letters. The halo keeps the glyph edges clean but doesn't stop that clutter. **Residual:** in batch-compact.png (standard text), the default top-right legend covers the white curve's broad maximum around 18–24°. Every sampled point scores the same (`legendPlacement.ts:31-38`), so a corner the main curve passes through with few samples can still beat a corner full of baseline samples. I count this as a minor heuristic limitation, not a regression: the legend can still be moved, and the narrow peaks and A cursor stay clear. |
| **Q2** Placement ignores cursor and ROI moves | **Fixed, documented as intentional** | A new comment (`PatternView.tsx:400-401`) says the legend corner stays fixed while you pan or move a cursor or ROI. I accept that as a deliberate stability choice. |
| **Q3** Clicking could pin the legend | **Fixed** | You now have to drag at least 4 px before the legend pins (`PlotLegend.tsx:170-181`); a plain click only focuses it. Arrow keys pin it and Home/Escape return it to automatic (`:137-156`, `:78-83`). Whether it's pinned is now shown in its ARIA description, `title` and `data-manual-position` attribute (`:124-126`). |
| **Q5** ASCII hyphen in bound messages | **Fixed** | Bound labels now use the Unicode minus (`numericDraft.ts:14-18`). |

**Regressions:** none I can substantiate. The halo color matches the plot background in the screenshots. Sampling now takes at most about 256 points per curve, and all transforms are monotonic, so keeping each bucket's extremes still ranks samples correctly.

## Reports and performance evidence

**Reports.** `after/report.json` and `webkit/report.json` both show `completed: true`, `failure: null` and `errors: []`. I verified these fields in the previous round, and nothing in this round suggested they had changed.

**New WebKit benchmark** (`performance/retina-webgl-webkit-current.json`):
- Renderer "Apple GPU", WebGL 2.0, DPR 2, one 2,000-point curve.
- The browser's refresh cadence was 20 ms. Every case (pan-wide, pan-zoomed, wheel) shows `missedFramePercent: 0`.
- Input-to-submission p95 is 5 ms or less, and frame interval p95 is 22 ms or less.
- Timings are reported in whole milliseconds.

**Provenance** (`performance/provenance.json`):
- Links the timings to 59fd3e38 "from the recorded session state". The raw benchmark files contain no commit hash, so this link rests on that file's word.
- Correctly says the later legend changes weren't timed.

**Native Batch smoke:** still unqualified, as you said. I didn't re-read its logs this round.

## Scores

**Responsiveness stays at 8.5.** The benchmark is meaningful new evidence: a real GPU, no missed frames, small input latency. Two things keep it below 9:
- **Scope:** it covers panning and zooming one 2,000-point curve. The criterion also covers "navigation and feedback … during scientific processing", plus raster and heatmap redraws, multi-curve overlays and processing-time feedback. None of those are measured.
- **Environment:** it ran in headless WebKit, not the Tauri app on a real display.

A benchmark that adds raster, Batch heatmap and multi-overlay cases, or measures UI response while integration is running, would justify 9.0.

**Visualization and readability stay at 9.0.** The Q1 residual is minor, and the user's movable in-plot legend design limits its impact.

**No other changes.** All 25 prior scores are retained, so the sum is 225.5 and the mean **225.5 / 25 = 9.02**.
